Turn pieces so matching inks touch: 6 joined hexes dissolve. Picture each turn in your mind first.
Imagery Competing Task Intervention
Right after something upsetting, your brain spends a few hours "saving" the memory, mostly as vivid pictures. A visual puzzle keeps that same part of your brain busy, so the memory gets stored less vividly and pops back into your head less often. You still remember what happened; it just intrudes less. It works best within about 6 hours, for 10 to 20 minutes, while really focusing on turning the pieces in your mind. Honeycomb's hexagons turn six ways, so they ask for a lot of mental rotation, which is the part that helps.
